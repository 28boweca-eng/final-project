Finding your Flight

When you get your boarding pass, it will have your gate number on it, as well as the flight number. The gate number will usually come in the form of a letter and a number (i.e. Gate A21). The letter stands for the terminal, while the number stands for the gate. When trying to find your gate, focus on getting to the correct terminal first. You can do this by looking at the airport maps, or asking an employee for directions. Once you get in the terminal, the gate numbers are all posted, which makes it easier to find your correct flight.

Sometimes, if your gate is further away, you will have to take one of the airport trains. They arrive at regular intervals, and can be boarded from inside the airport. When entering the train, calmly walk inside, and either sit down in one of the seats, or hold on to a handrail. The train will announce when and where it will be stopping, so pay attention so that you don’t miss your stop. Also, don’t try to enter or exit the train when the doors close and the train announces it will be leaving.

Once you are at your gate, feel free to sit in one of the seats in or around your gate until it is announced that your flight is boarding. However, if you are in a crowded area, don't put your bag on one of the seats. This makes it so that some people can't sit down, and will annoy everyone.

Stores, Restaurants, and Other Places to Stop

In the terminal, there are many small airport markets that sell snacks, as well as beverages, toiletrees, and souveniers. These stores, while convienient, are super overpriced, and they are designed to profit off of new travelers. If you want snacks on the plane, be sure to stop at a grocery store beforhand and bring them in your personal bag.

There will also be many restaurants and fast food places in the terminal. Just like the airport stores, these restaurants are overpriced, and oftentimes aren't the best quality. However, these are the only real places to get an actual hot meal inside of an airport, instead of just snacks. If you have some extra money and really want to go to a restaurant, then go ahead. Otherwise, just stick to your snacks that you hopefully bought before coming to the airport.

Of course, there are also bathrooms. These bathrooms are anything but sensory friendly, and I know from personal experience that they are very good at causing meltdowns. To prevent yourself, your family member, or your friend from totally losing it in the bathrooms, be sure to have headphones or earplugs ready. If you are smaller, then put toilet paper over the toilet sensor to prevent it from flushing while you are using it. Lastly, if hand driers or hot water bothers you, just use hand sanitizer instead of washing your hands at the sinks.

There are many other places in the airport, such as breast feeding stations, service animal relief areas, quiet spaces, and occasionally even a library. Be sure to look at the airport map so that you know where these places are and how to get to them in case you need to.

Airport Assistance

If you are a wheelchair user traveling alone, have difficultly reading signs, or just need any form of help, contact the airport customer service at least 48 hours before your flight to help you. These workers are here to assist people like you with their travels, and will make sure that you will reach your flight on time.

Boarding the Plane

Once the gate agent has announced that the flight is now boarding, look at your boarding pass again. Check to see your seat number, and which zone it says. The agent will periodically call up zone numbers, as well as priority boarding and boarding for veterans and people with physical disabilities. Wait until your zone is called before going to gate agent.

When the agent calls out your zone number, get in the line up to the agent's desk. Just like security, these lines can be long, depending on the time of day and year. If the line is long, your only choice is unfortunately to just wait it out. As soon as you reach the agent, show them your boarding pass, let them scan it, and move into the boarding area once they give you the heads up to do so.

As you are walking in the queue to board the plane, keep moving with everyone else. If you have to stop for any reason, move to the side so that you are not preventing others from getting on the plane. Of course, if the line for the gate agent was long, the line for the queue will also be long. Just like in any other long line in the airport, your only choice is to just wait it out.
